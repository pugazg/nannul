# Nannūl Grammatical-Terminology Review Guidelines

## Purpose

This document governs human review of the mechanically discovered grammatical-terminology candidates in:

`data/grammatical-terminology-candidates.json`

The discovery layer is intentionally broad. Frequency, recurrence across sections, or appearance in a source heading is **evidence for review priority only** and is never an automatic claim that an exact surface form is a grammatical technical term.

## Source authority

Review must use the repository's completed source-derived canonical Tamil layer and stable நூற்பா IDs as the primary evidence base.

Do not silently:

- rewrite or normalize source spelling;
- strip source punctuation;
- merge exact surface forms merely because they look related;
- reconstruct source gaps 73 or 176;
- import a historical-mirror reading into the controlling current-source edition;
- infer a technical sense from frequency alone.

Commentaries, Tolkāppiyam, later grammar works, dictionaries, or scholarship may be consulted in later analytical phases, but their evidence must be identified separately from what Nannūl itself supports.

## Generated discovery data is read-only

Do **not** hand-edit:

- `data/grammatical-terminology-candidates.json`;
- `data/grammatical-terminology-candidates.ndjson`;
- `indexes/grammatical-terminology-review-queue.md`.

They are regenerated by `scripts/generate_terminology_candidates.py`.

Human decisions belong in a separate review ledger so regeneration never destroys editorial judgment.

## Stable candidate identity

Each exact surface form receives a candidate ID of the form:

`nannul-term-candidate-<16 hex>`

The suffix is the first 16 hexadecimal characters of SHA-256 over the exact UTF-8 `surface_form_ta`.

Therefore:

- candidate identity is tied to the exact surface form;
- candidate identity does not depend on frequency or review rank;
- review rank may change when evidence changes without invalidating a prior review decision;
- differently punctuated or differently spelled source forms remain distinct candidates unless an explicit later analytical relationship is recorded.

## Review decisions

A reviewer may assign exactly one of:

### `accepted`

Use only when the reviewed Nannūl contexts support treating the exact surface form as a grammatical/metalanguage term in at least one documented context.

An accepted decision must include:

- concise rationale;
- at least one stable நூற்பா ID as supporting evidence;
- a proposed analytical category;
- whether the technical sense appears context-specific or broadly grammatical.

Acceptance applies to the **reviewed exact surface form and documented contexts**. It does not automatically merge inflected, compounded, punctuated, or orthographic variants.

### `rejected`

Use when the reviewed evidence shows that the discovery hit should not be indexed as a grammatical technical term for this analytical layer—for example a connective, ordinary lexical item, incidental example word, purely numerical form, or another false positive.

A rejected decision must include a rationale and at least one reviewed context.

### `needs-context`

Use when the available occurrences are ambiguous, polysemous, or insufficient for a defensible accepted/rejected judgment.

Record what additional contextual or witness evidence is needed.

## Proposed analytical categories

Categories are editorial metadata, not source text. Initial categories may include:

- `meta-grammar`
- `phonology-letter-class`
- `phonology-sound-process`
- `orthography`
- `morphology`
- `case`
- `noun`
- `verb`
- `particle`
- `syntax`
- `semantics`
- `prosodic-measure`
- `textual-pedagogy`
- `other-grammar`

This list may be revised as review proceeds. Category changes must not alter canonical Tamil or stable candidate identity.

## Context review minimum

For each candidate, inspect:

1. the first occurrence;
2. all source-heading evidence, if present;
3. occurrences across distinct இயல்/structural units;
4. enough local canonical நூற்பா context to distinguish technical use from ordinary use;
5. any occurrence linked to a documented source variant.

For very frequent forms, representative contexts may be reviewed first, but acceptance should not claim uniform technical meaning across every occurrence unless that has actually been checked.

## Polysemy and mixed use

A surface form may be grammatical in some contexts and ordinary in others.

Do not force a whole-form binary interpretation when the evidence is mixed. The review ledger should record:

- `term_use_record_ids` for positively supported technical contexts;
- `non_term_use_record_ids` when known;
- notes describing ambiguity or mixed use.

## External comparison boundary

Tolkāppiyam, commentaries, Nannūl editions, dictionaries, and later grammar works can strengthen later interpretation, but such evidence must be tagged as external/secondary analytical evidence.

The first review pass should preferentially establish what can be supported internally from Nannūl itself.

## Review batching

Review in ranked batches rather than attempting all candidates at once.

Recommended first pass:

- start with candidates carrying exact source-heading evidence;
- inspect high-priority candidates in groups of roughly 20–30;
- record decisions in the separate review ledger;
- audit each completed batch;
- never infer decisions for the remaining unreviewed candidates.

## Completion standard

The terminology layer is not complete merely because candidate discovery is complete.

Completion requires an explicit reviewed ledger with every candidate in a declared state (`accepted`, `rejected`, or `needs-context`) and an audit showing decision coverage and provenance.
